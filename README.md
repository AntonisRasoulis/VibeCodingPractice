# Fire Run

A 2-player pixel-art race on one keyboard (or 1 player against the fire). The screen is split in two: Red runs on top, Blue on the bottom, through the exact same endless course: the prairie, or with biomes on, snow, desert and jungle too. A wall of fire chases each of you and keeps getting faster. If it catches you, the other player wins.

Built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No frameworks or build tools are needed.

## Controls

| | Jump | Run | Use your power (wind dash, lightning, hand, drone...) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Red** (top) | `W` | `A` / `D` | `S` |
| **Blue** (bottom) | `↑` | `←` / `→` | `↓` |

**1 Player:** pick it on the start screen to race the fire alone with WASD or the arrow keys. It shows one big view. Only the orbs that help you on your own appear: dash, wind dash and drone, plus each biome's own two (the monkey, firehawk and snowball turn into helpful versions) and the purple portal gun and vine; swap, freeze, lightning, hand and grapple need a second player. Your best distance is remembered (one best for Normal, one for Wild).

**Mode:** the Mode button under the start buttons switches between **Normal** and **Wild**, the hard mode (see below).

`P` or `Esc` pauses. `M` (or the Sound button) turns the sound off and on.

## Run it locally

Open `index.html` in your browser. Or start a local server:

```
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then visit http://localhost:8000.

## How the endless level works

- **One level, two cameras.** There's only one level in memory. Each half of the screen follows one player through it, which is why both courses are always identical.
- **Built in sections.** The level is stitched together from hand-designed pieces: flat ground, steps, spikes, pits, floating platforms, moving platforms, crumbling platforms, swinging maces, spike shooters, saw blades, a portal shortcut and swap orbs. The prairie also has bear traps and bushes (`TRAP` and `BUSH` in config.js): traps lie flat in the grass; small and medium ones hold you until you wiggle free (left, right, left... `TRAP.wiggles`: 4 and 8 presses; holding one way doesn't count), large ones (only after 400 m) kill. Running into a trapped player knocks them free and traps you instead. A trap that has snapped stays shut for everyone. Inside a bush you are hidden on both screens; about 10% of bushes hide a trap, and when it snaps the bush is torn open and stops hiding anyone. Besides their own sections, traps and bushes are sprinkled all over the prairie (`SCATTER`): before pits, near saws and maces, between other dangers. So you never get stuck, a trap only goes where it can always be jumped over (5 tiles of flat ground each side, nothing overhead), never takes the last safe place to stand between two dangers, and the deadly large ones stay out of reach of other dangers; bushes never cover spikes, pits or saw slots. A test jumps every trap on 150 generated courses, from full speed and from a standing start.
- **Thin ice** (`THIN_ICE`, snow) is the snow's trap: pale patches over a pond, 7-12 tiles long (too long to jump over: walk across it). It cracks the first time anyone walks on it (the cracks spread from each footstep and stay); it holds while you're on it, but stepping back onto cracked ice breaks it, and so do a jump onto or off it and both players standing on it at once. Every snow biome gets at least 2-3 patches (`THIN_ICE.perBiome`). Breaking through freezes you in a block of ice in the water; 3 presses of jump break you out. Everyone frozen in the same pond shares one block: the first out survives and the rest shatter. The open water stays for both players and freezes anyone who walks in. Placed in its own sections and scattered over the snow with the same never-get-stuck rules as the traps.
- **Desert sinkholes** (`SINK`) are the desert's bear traps: little holes, barely darker than the sand. Step (on the ground) too close and a small or medium one opens into a funnel and pulls you to the bottom; the sand pours in, but stops for 0.2 s every 0.9 s (it sparkles): jump then to get out. A jump at the wrong moment makes you slip back for 0.5 s, so mashing doesn't work. A large one (after 400 m) caves in to a 5-tile gap: jump over the hole or fall. Opened sinkholes stay open for both players (a caved-in one is a real pit). They get their own sections and are scattered over the desert with the same never-get-stuck rules as the traps (and never in a tumbleweed's path); a test jumps over or climbs out of every sinkhole on 100 desert courses. Saw blades come in three sizes (`SAW` in config.js) and slide along a slot in the ground, go up and down a rail (rising right through where a jump takes you, so you can't just always jump them), or rise out of a pit; their speeds were measured with a simulated runner so every saw always leaves a way through. Each piece is designed so it can always be cleared, and the pieces get harder further along.
- **Built just ahead of the leader.** New sections are added on the right whenever the player in front gets close to the end, so the course never runs out.
- **Biomes.** They start out turned off, so new players learn the game in the prairie; the **Biomes** button under the start buttons turns them on (remembered by the browser, `fire-run-biomes`). With them on, an arrow appears next to it: it opens a row of biome toggles to choose which biomes the race goes through (remembered as `fire-run-biome-list`), and under them a row of **secret biomes**, the ones reached in special ways: for now the enchanted forest (remembered as `fire-run-secret-list`; on by default). At least one chip of either row stays on. With the forest off there are no sleep mushrooms (`Level.sleepShroomsOn`); with every regular biome off, the whole race is in the secret biome(s), played like any biome on the main course: all enchanted forest (no sleep mushrooms or firefly exits, so no magic feet or roots), all arcane ruins (no portal, no 20 s timer, no exit to reach, no collapse or falling stones, so no aurora; its fire turrets, eye-stones and gear lasers as usual, gear lasers, eye-stones and saws only after the first 60 tiles; orbs as in other biomes, about 12 weight), or both, taking turns at signs (a purple wood sign with a glowing mushroom for the forest, a stone slab with a teal rune for the ruins). Bot races: all ruins, 1 player reached 1250 m (4 deaths), 2 players 1290 and 1245 m; forest and ruins, 768 m alone, 879 and 854 m for two; no errors. With Biomes off it's the prairie, with sleep mushrooms, as before. With only one picked, the whole race stays in that biome, like the prairie with biomes off. With biomes on, every 250-450 tiles (about 350 on average) a sign marks a new biome. The first race after opening the website starts in the prairie; later races start in a random biome. In the **jungle** (`JUNGLE`), the spikes are thin bamboo stakes 20-32 px tall (a jump lifts your feet about 38 px; `Level.spikeKind` remembers each one) or low thorn bushes 2-5 tiles wide; a test jumps every one on 40 jungle courses from full speed and from a standing start. Jungle **rivers** (`RIVER`, small 8-12, medium 13-19 or big 20-28 tiles) are crossed on logs that drift left with the current: they bob up at the far bank and sink at the near one, worked out from the clock like the moving platforms so both players see the same logs. Fall in and the current carries you back to the near bank (no death, just lost time). Each river's log length, gap and speed are picked so there's always a way across with time to spare: on a wide river the next log is up and steady a good second before yours reaches the near bank; on a narrow one a single log stays within an easy 4-tile hop of both banks for at least 2.5 s (the current slows down if nothing else fits). A test crosses every river on 30 courses, 4 times each, playing like a person. **Palm trees** (`COCONUT`, `PALMS`) are common, in 5 kinds (classic, leaning, royal, fan and windswept), all hung with coconuts; about one in five has one that falls. It starts wobbling when you run within 72 px of it (its shadow grows on the ground as it falls) and drops 0.35 s later: keep running flat out and it bonks you about half the time (dizzy and stuck for 1 s), ease off as it wobbles and it never does. Like the icicles, each player gets their own, and a falling one is never placed where being stunned would leave you in another danger's reach. **Flytraps** (`FLYTRAP`) are the jungle's trap, shared by both players. One never closes on its own: get within its sense range and it rears back for 0.35 s, then snaps at everything in its bite (12-16 px either side of it and 44 px up, higher than any jump). The sense range is worked out from the wind-up so that running straight in at full speed always gets you bitten, but backing off as soon as it rears (within about a quarter of a second) always makes it miss. After a miss it chews on nothing for 1.2 s and takes 0.35 s to open: time to run past. A victim is chewed for 1.5 s (legs kicking out of the top); then a small green one spits them back about 5 tiles (dazed for 0.8 s) and a big purple one (only after 400 m) swallows them. Each needs 9 tiles of flat, open, safe ground both sides (to bait it from and land on). A test in the real game checks that running in and jumping at any distance always gets you eaten, and faking it out works. Wild: a 0.28 s wind-up and 0.9 s of chewing on nothing, and more big ones. **Ancient turrets** (`TURRET`) are stone idol heads buried flush with the jungle floor, with nothing showing: when someone comes within 260 px of one ahead (or has run 16-40 px past one behind, about half of them), the ground rumbles for 0.45 s and it grinds up and shoots arrows along the ground. For a regular shot it rises 20 px in 0.35 s, the tip glows for 0.45 s and the arrow flies at body height at 420 px/s; for a low one it rises 16 px in 0.2 s, glows for just 0.2 s and fires at 520 px/s, skimming your feet. A jump clears either. Arrows drop as they fly (`TURRET.drop`) and stick in the ground where they land (a regular one about 270 px out, a low one about 200), harmless, then fade. Before 400 m every arrow is a plain one (red, kills you); after that it's any of plain, poison (green: 6 s of your view wobbling, doubling and changing color, worse and worse, then clearing; it never hurts you), sleep (blue: 3 s asleep on the spot) or blunt (yellow knob: thrown back like the hand's backhand, about 25 tiles, and dizzy). One fires 1-2 arrows, then sinks back for good. Its arrows are picked when it wakes up and fired in order: sleep darts first (so the next arrow finds you asleep), blunt ones last (knocking you out of reach of the rest); it only takes another shot at someone still at least 60 px from its mouth (never point-blank). Turrets are shared, and their arrows hit anyone in the way. Each sits in its own section of flat ground covering where its arrows fly, which counts as threatened, so nothing else is placed there. Tests in the real game: jumping 0-0.4 s after a low shot's glow (0.15-0.65 s for a regular one) always dodges it, and runners that jump the arrows are never hit in Normal; in Wild they need short hops to get through the quicker follow-up shots. In the **taiga** (the snow biome, `snow` in the code), spikes are taller ice spikes, ice patches are slippery (`SNOW.iceAccel`, `SNOW.iceFriction`), icicles under snowy ledges shake and drop when you get close, and snowballs roll at you and grow (up to 17 px across, `SNOW.snowballMaxR`, so they can always be jumped). Like the crumbling platforms, each player gets their own icicles and snowballs.
- **Desert.** Spikes become cactuses (14-18 px tall, only in groups of 1-2). Quicksand pits: you sink in 3 s; about 6 quick jump presses (4+ a second) pull you free, slow pressing or wading doesn't. Tumbleweeds roll at you bouncing up and down. Settings in `DESERT` in config.js.
- **Special dangers start late.** Nothing biome-special comes before 400 m. From 400 m: black missiles, brown and black bears, brown vultures. From 500 m: red-striped missiles, polar bears and black vultures too (`THREATS` in config.js).
- **Wild mode** (`WILD` in config.js) is the hard mode, picked with the Mode button (remembered by the browser, `fire-run-mode`). Everything above comes from the very start (no 400 m / 500 m wait). Instead of a couple per biome, a missile comes for the leader the whole time they're in the prairie, the next one as soon as the last has exploded or flown off (and a death doesn't send an extra one). In the desert a new group of vultures comes 0.6 s after the last one is gone, and in the snow a new bear (any kind) charges in 0.5 s after the last one gives up, so there's always one after you; in the jungle a new jaguar or panther comes 0.6 s after the last one gives up, and turrets fire 3 arrows with less warning (0.3 s, or 0.14 s for a low shot) and less time between them. Flytraps rear back for 0.28 s instead of 0.35 and chew on nothing for 0.9 s instead of 1.2. Saws, maces and spike shooters move 15% faster (`WILD.speedUp`), large traps and sinkholes are twice as likely as each smaller size (`WILD.bigOdds`), and quicksand swallows you in 1.6 s with presses wearing off faster, so it takes about 6 presses a second to get out (Normal: about 4). The same never-get-stuck rules and tests apply: every saw still has a way through, and a simulated runner gets through Wild courses as often as Normal ones. In 1 player, Wild keeps its own best distance (`fire-run-best-wild`).
- **Each biome has one special danger.** The jungle has **jaguars** (`JAGUAR`): once or twice per jungle biome (from 400 m; black panthers from 500 m too) its small glowing eyes fade in (over 0.8 s) in one of the low bushes all through the jungle's background, 170-210 px ahead of the leader, and 1.8 s after they start to appear they narrow and then it leaps out of the background straight at the leader, following them through the air (changing speed doesn't help; its shadow follows you), comes in low and lunges right through where they are: jump as it lunges and it passes under you. Then it runs at you and attacks: crouched flat it pounces low (jump it), rear and tail up it leaps over you (keep running; jumping into it is what gets you). A jaguar attacks 3 times, a panther once but faster. It never springs from closer than 36 px (if you're running at it, it backs off first), so you always get a fair look at its crouch. If you die to something else it waits just in front of where you'll come back. Every 4-10 s, harmless decoy eyes fade in, blink and fade out in some other bush, so you can't be sure; only a jaguar's eyes narrow. A test in the real game has a player who reacts like a person (0.2-0.3 s) survive every jaguar and panther in 2 minutes of Wild, while one who never stops or always jumps dies to nearly every one. The desert has **vultures**: about every 200 m, a group of 1-3 comes for the leader. Brown ones circle for 2 s, then swoop and skim the ground (jump over them) and snatch you back about 13 tiles if they hit. Black ones dive straight in, faster, and kill. If you die while they're still circling (or haven't come in yet), they don't give up: they find where you respawn and carry on, their countdown paused while you're gone. Settings in `VULTURE`. The prairie has the missiles. The snow has **bears**: twice per snow biome (at least 70 m apart, and never two chasing at once) a bear (randomly polar, brown or black) charges in from the left after the leader. Running right nonstop always stays just ahead. Polar: fastest, 5 s, any slowdown is deadly. Brown: 10 s, a short stop is OK. Black: slowest, 15 s, catches you with honey (slowed for 4 s) instead of killing. If the player it's chasing dies to something else, the bear runs (forwards or backwards) to just behind where they'll respawn, waits in view, and carries on the chase; its clock keeps running, so if time runs out while it waits, it gives up. It waits as close as `BEAR.waitBehind` but never so close that it could catch someone running flat out before its time is up. A bear never pops into view on the trailing player's screen: if they're close enough to see where it starts, it starts off the left of their screen instead, charges through (launching them into the air if it runs over them) and only starts its timed chase once it reaches the leader's screen. Tune them in `BEAR` in config.js.
- **Missiles aren't part of the course.** After 400 m, in the prairie only. Each prairie biome gets 2 missiles 75% of the time (1 missile 15%, 3 missiles 10%, `MISSILE.perBiome`), spread out across it; with biomes off, every 350 m of prairie counts as one biome. A missile comes when the leader reaches its spot, and only one is ever in the air at a time. If the player a missile is coming for dies from something else, the missile doesn't go away or wait: it stays where it is and fires on time, at the spot where they died if they're still dead, or at them if they've already respawned. And a fresh missile comes for them once they're back (dying to a missile never sends another). It zooms in from the background on the left or right of a player, locks on for 0.6 s (missiles only ever come for the player in the lead), then fires in a straight line. Red-striped ones (after 500 m) aim where the player is heading.
- **Per-player crumbling platforms.** Each player has their own copy of the crumbling platforms. If one falls under Red, it's still there when Blue arrives.
- **Jungle orbs** (`MONSOON`, `MONKEY`): only placed in the jungle, about 15% of its orbs each. The gold monsoon works at once: for 10 s (rising over 1.2 s, draining over 1.2 s) the water rises to 30 px from the top of the screen, above every obstacle, with logs (4-5 tiles, 3-4 tile gaps) drifting left on it at 42 px/s once it's up. A log rises up under everyone's feet and lifts them. While it's flooded nothing can hurt you (`Player.die` does nothing and the hazards aren't checked), the animals, missiles and arrows are washed away, fires are put out, and the other dangers wait. Off a log you're swept left at 240 px/s for at least 0.8 s (about 24 tiles), then catch the first log you come to. As it drains, a log with someone on it carries them down onto the nearest safe ground within 60 tiles. Tests on 15 real jungle courses: standing still on your log or falling in, nobody died; a runner hopping from log to log got about 90 tiles further. The light blue monkey (S / Down) swings in on a vine from above the top of the screen (100 px radius, 0.7 s, fastest at the bottom) aimed at where the other player will be; it can only catch them near the bottom of the swing, so jumping it dodges it. Caught, they're swept up and flung back about 6 tiles. Either way it then chases them at running speed (giving up after 8 s); once it reaches them it sits on their head and covers their screen with two big hands for 4 s (`Player.blind`). Alone, it swings you forward to the next safe spot at least 8 tiles ahead (13 tiles in a test).
- **The sleep mushroom and the enchanted forest** (`SLEEP_SHROOM`, `FOREST`, `SPORES`): each race gets exactly one giant sleep mushroom: one chance at the forest (`Level.sleepAt`, a random spot in a biome, never before 400 m: `SLEEP_SHROOM.from`; if a biome's spot comes too early, the next biome tries again, and once it's placed there are no more: `Level.sleepPlaced`). Over 400 races with all four biomes: always exactly one in the first 3000 m, at 414-1170 m (median 576 m, the middle half 508-658 m); with biomes off, 414-936 m (median 548 m). From the mushroom to the forest's way out (where the fireflies take you, with its gifts) is 121-306 m, 206 m on average. It looks like a spore mushroom, a bit bigger and purple. Its puffs are worked out from the clock (`Level.sleepPhase`): 3-10 s on, then 10 s off. While it puffs, 12 spores a second drop from under its cap and float down at 14 px/s, drifting 9 px/s to one side (`Level.sleepSpores`), so the rain takes about 2 s to reach the ground; a spore touching you puts you to sleep (`updateSleepShrooms`). Running through once the rain is down: asleep 30 times out of 30. The forest is a separate `Level` (biome `forest`, `realmEnd` set) covering the same x as the main course from where the mushroom stands, 100-250 tiles long, shared by everyone who sleeps at that mushroom. A player in it has `realm` set: they update on that level and see it on their screen, and while the main course's dangers update, anyone in a forest (or on their way between) counts as `caught` for a moment (`onCourse`), so nothing there targets them. Once you reach the calm ground at its end (`Level.realmExit`: the first section built past `realmEnd`, so never in the middle of an obstacle), fireflies swarm round you (1.3 s) and you wake back on the main course at the nearest safe spot (`moveTo`). The fire is the same fire everywhere. In the forest: 1 in 5 saws breaks loose (once it would land with 12 tiles of clear ground to roll on) and rolls at the nearest runner at 140 px/s. The forest has its own mix of sections (`FOREST.mix`), balanced so everything gets its turn without any forest being promised anything. Its sections sit closer together than on the main course (`FOREST.gap`: 0-1 flat tiles between them, against 1-3; `treeRoom`, `lobberRoom`: 6/7 tiles round an alive tree, 3/4 round a Lobber): over 300 forests, plain ground with nothing on it went from 55% of the forest to 42%, and the stretches of it between dangers from 11 tiles on average to 6.8 (median 6), while every one of 2621 pairs of dangers still had a safe spot to wait between them. Over 500 forests (184 tiles on average, about 7.8 sections, before the tighter spacing): bouncy gaps 1.08 per forest (32% of forests have none), Lobbers 0.92 (40%), spore mushrooms 0.91 (43%), alive trees 0.90 (39%), saws 0.95 (53%; 0.21 break loose), maces 0.83 (55%; 0.17 break off), floating shooters 1.08 (0.42 single, 0.26 stacks; 50% none), rotten-branch sections 0.51 (60%), spike groups 0.32, pits 0.30, moving platforms 0.25, floating platforms 0.24, steps 0.23, portals 0.15. **Breaking maces:** 1 in 5 of its maces (`FOREST.maceBreaks`) snaps off its vine: once a runner is within 150 px, the vine glows and flickers, a crack of light where it'll break (0.6 s), then, as soon as its swing is heading their way (or after 1.5 s anyway), the ball is flung at the nearest runner, in an arc: half the time (`FOREST.maceAhead`) at where they'll be when it lands (their speed times its flight, if they're moving faster than 20 px/s), otherwise at where they are, that falls with gravity (420 px/s²), landing 0.55-0.95 s later; of those throws it picks the one most in line with its swing. Where it lands it lies there, glowing, a spiked ball to jump over (anywhere next to it counts as threatened, so nobody respawns on it); down a pit, it's gone; a stub of vine keeps swinging. Tests (40 courses each): running straight on, hit 17 times in 40 (about half the throws lead you); standing still where you'd wait for a mace, 33 in 40; stopping as soon as it's flung, 23 in 40; reading the arc (stop if it's heading ahead of you, keep running if it's coming at you), 1 in 40; stopping while the vine glows, then stepping back once it's flung, 0 in 40 (going forwards instead, 6 in 40); spike shooters float 2-10 tiles each way and can be ridden, sometimes stacked 2-3 high; its crumbling platforms are rotten branches that don't fall: stand on one for 0.175 s in all (`FOREST.branchHold`; stepping off doesn't reset it) and it snaps in two and you drop straight down. A crack spreads from its middle while you stand on it, and it creaks and shakes for the last 0.075 s. Each player has their own (like the crumbling platforms), and they come back when you respawn. Too quick to run along one (a whole branch takes 0.33 s): hop straight back off each branch as you land (the 0.1 s jump buffer helps). In tests (40 courses of three branch sections each), a runner jumping from the bank 28-36 px before its edge and hopping off every branch the moment it landed crossed all 40 (0.008 s on each branch); the window for that take-off is 8-16 px wide (about 0.1-0.17 s). The playtest bot, which plans one jump at a time, crossed 32 of 40 without dying and waited on the bank on the rest; with magic feet you stand on the magic instead; about 0.9 spore mushrooms per forest (red 1.4x speed, blue 1.5x jump height, green poison, 10 s; pink: float up at 14 px/s for 5 s), caught about 9 times in 10 when running under one. Its obstacles have their own look (mossy logs, glowing mushroom caps, rotten branches, thorn wheels, seed-pod maces, hollow stumps). No orbs in the forest. **Alive trees** (`ALIVE_TREE`, `Level.trees`, about 0.9 per forest): they look just like the twisting trees in the background, but stand on the course and sway a little, a branch twitching now and then. It always strikes right at its trunk: when a runner comes within reach (worked out from the wind-up, like the flytrap's: running straight in at full speed, you reach the trunk just as the root comes up), the ground at its foot bulges up for 0.4 s (a mound of earth rising and spreading, shaking for the last part, cracks splitting it and pebbles hopping on it, until the root's tip pokes through the top), then a sharp root shoots up there, 42 px tall (no jumping it), hitting 4 px either side: "SKEWERED!", you die. It bursts out (the mound splitting and slumping outwards over 0.15 s, `ALIVE_TREE.burst`, clods of earth flung out to land round the hole, where they lie until it closes) and stays up 0.35 s, then shakes as it sinks back over 0.4 s, earth crumbling off it into the hole; then over 0.9 s (`ALIVE_TREE.close`) the clods and the slumped mound slide back and drop into the hole, the hole fills in from its edges, and the grass grows back over the turned earth a pixel at a time (nothing just fades away); then the tree rests 1.2 s: stop short when the ground bulges, then run past. Each tree's first attack is a grab instead: quicker (0.32 s of bulging) and wider (12 px either side), one thick root catches you ("GRABBED!"), flings you 30 px up, coils round you, then yanks you down into the dirt, and you wake up on the main course (like a blue Lobber mushroom). Spots near an alive tree count as threatened, so nobody comes back there. Tests on courses with two trees: running straight on, grabbed at the first tree 30 times in 30 (skewered 30 in 30 once their grabs are used); stopping when the cracks appear (seeing them 0.12 s late) and going once the root is down, through both trees 30 in 30, grab or root. **Mushroom Lobbers** (`LOBBER`, `Level.lobbers`, 0.92 per forest over 500 forests, 40% have none): a lumpy lilac puffball on a short stem with a purple pore on its upper front, on open flat ground. It leans toward the nearest runner within 260 px and once a second squashes down flat and lobs a little glowing mushroom out of its pore, up and over in a high arc (0.95-1.15 s in the air, climbing 60-85 px whether you're near or far), aimed where you are, where you'll be (your speed times its flight) or 26 px behind you (equally likely). Red (3 in 10) kills you ("SPLAT!"); green (3 in 10) poisons you ("POISONED!", the screen wobbles, doubles and changes color worse and worse) and kills you 3 s later (another green doesn't restart the countdown); pink (3 in 10) floats you up at 110 px/s and you die once you're off the top of the screen (0.9 s; or after 2.5 s if something's in the way); blue (1 in 10) wakes you up ("WOKEN UP!": the screen goes dark and you wake on the main course at the same distance, with none of the forest's gifts). Lobs hit anyone they touch on the way, and pop harmlessly on the ground; the Lobber itself is harmless to touch. Tests: running straight past one, hit 31 times in 40; the playtest bot, seeing each lob 0.15 s after it's thrown and dodging (speeding up, stopping or jumping), 0 times in 40, just as fast. **Bouncy mushrooms** (`BOUNCY`, `Level.bouncies`, about 1.08 gaps per forest, 32% of forests have none): some of the forest's wide gaps have 2-4 giant orange mushrooms growing up out of them on long twisting stems (each winds in an S with at least two curves). Land on one and it bounces you up: at 90% of the speed you came down, at least 240 px/s (a jump is 265), at most 330, so bouncing on the spot stays steady and waiting is always possible. Press jump just as you land (within the 0.1 s jump buffer; holding it down doesn't count) for a super bounce, 1.35 times higher ("SUPER BOUNCE!"): you spin and can't steer until you land on solid ground (a still runner is flung the way they face).
