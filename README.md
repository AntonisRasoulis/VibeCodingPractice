# Fire Run

A 2-player pixel-art race on one keyboard. The screen is split in two: Red runs on top, Blue on the bottom, through the exact same endless prairie. A wall of fire chases each of you and keeps getting faster. If it catches you, the other player wins.

Built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No frameworks or build tools are needed.

## Controls

| | Jump | Run | Use ability (wind dash / lightning / hand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Red** (top) | `W` | `A` / `D` | `S` |
| **Blue** (bottom) | `↑` | `←` / `→` | `↓` |

**1 Player:** pick it on the start screen to race the fire alone with WASD or the arrow keys. It shows one big view, only dash, wind dash and drone orbs appear (the others need a second player), and your best distance is remembered (one best for Normal, one for Wild).

**Mode:** the Mode button under the start buttons switches between **Normal** and **Wild**, the hard mode (see below).

`P` or `Esc` pauses. `M` (or the Sound button) turns the sound off and on.

## Run it locally

Open `index.html` in your browser. Or start a local server:

```
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then visit http://localhost:8000.

## Project structure

```
index.html            The page: header, game area, how-to-play, footer
css/style.css         Styling for the page
js/main.js            Connects the page (Start/Pause buttons, win counters) to the game
js/game/config.js     Numbers you can tweak: speeds, jump height, fire speed, respawn time
js/game/sound.js      Sound effects, made by the browser's synthesizer (no sound files)
js/game/level.js      The endless level generator
js/game/player.js     A runner: running, jumping, collisions, dying and respawning
js/game/sprites.js    The pixel art (drawn as text, one letter per pixel)
js/game/render.js     Draws everything
js/game/game.js       The game loop: keyboard, fire, swap orbs, who wins
```

## How the endless level works

- **One level, two cameras.** There's only one level in memory. Each half of the screen follows one player through it, which is why both courses are always identical.
- **Built in sections.** The level is stitched together from hand-designed pieces: flat ground, steps, spikes, pits, floating platforms, moving platforms, crumbling platforms, swinging maces, spike shooters, saw blades, a portal shortcut and swap orbs. The prairie also has bear traps and bushes (`TRAP` and `BUSH` in config.js): traps lie flat in the grass; small and medium ones hold you until you wiggle free (left, right, left... `TRAP.wiggles`: 4 and 8 presses; holding one way doesn't count), large ones (only after 400 m) kill. Running into a trapped player knocks them free and traps you instead. A trap that has snapped stays shut for everyone. Inside a bush you are hidden on both screens; about 10% of bushes hide a trap, and when it snaps the bush is torn open and stops hiding anyone. Besides their own sections, traps and bushes are sprinkled all over the prairie (`SCATTER`): before pits, near saws and maces, between other dangers. So you never get stuck, a trap only goes where it can always be jumped over (5 tiles of flat ground each side, nothing overhead), never takes the last safe place to stand between two dangers, and the deadly large ones stay out of reach of other dangers; bushes never cover spikes, pits or saw slots. A test jumps every trap on 150 generated courses, from full speed and from a standing start.
- **Thin ice** (`THIN_ICE`, snow) is the snow's trap: pale patches over a pond, 7-12 tiles long (too long to jump over: walk across it). It cracks the first time anyone walks on it (the cracks spread from each footstep and stay); it holds while you're on it, but stepping back onto cracked ice breaks it, and so do a jump onto or off it and both players standing on it at once. Every snow biome gets at least 2-3 patches (`THIN_ICE.perBiome`). Breaking through freezes you in a block of ice in the water; 3 presses of jump break you out. Everyone frozen in the same pond shares one block: the first out survives and the rest shatter. The open water stays for both players and freezes anyone who walks in. Placed in its own sections and scattered over the snow with the same never-get-stuck rules as the traps.
- **Desert sinkholes** (`SINK`) are the desert's bear traps: little holes, barely darker than the sand. Step (on the ground) too close and a small or medium one opens into a funnel and pulls you to the bottom; the sand pours in, but stops for 0.2 s every 0.9 s (it sparkles): jump then to get out. A jump at the wrong moment makes you slip back for 0.5 s, so mashing doesn't work. A large one (after 400 m) caves in to a 5-tile gap: jump over the hole or fall. Opened sinkholes stay open for both players (a caved-in one is a real pit). They get their own sections and are scattered over the desert with the same never-get-stuck rules as the traps (and never in a tumbleweed's path); a test jumps over or climbs out of every sinkhole on 100 desert courses. Saw blades come in three sizes (`SAW` in config.js) and slide along a slot in the ground, go up and down a rail (rising right through where a jump takes you, so you can't just always jump them), or rise out of a pit; their speeds were measured with a simulated runner so every saw always leaves a way through. Each piece is designed so it can always be cleared, and the pieces get harder further along.
- **Built just ahead of the leader.** New sections are added on the right whenever the player in front gets close to the end, so the course never runs out.
- **Biomes.** They start out turned off, so new players learn the game in the prairie; the **Biomes** button under the start buttons turns them on (remembered by the browser, `fire-run-biomes`). With them on, an arrow appears next to it: it opens a row of biome toggles to choose which biomes the race goes through (at least one; remembered as `fire-run-biome-list`). With only one picked, the whole race stays in that biome, like the prairie with biomes off. With biomes on, every 250-450 tiles (about 350 on average) a sign marks a new biome. The first race after opening the website starts in the prairie; later races start in a random biome. In the **jungle** (`JUNGLE`), the spikes are thin bamboo stakes 20-32 px tall (a jump lifts your feet about 38 px; `Level.spikeKind` remembers each one) or low thorn bushes 2-5 tiles wide; a test jumps every one on 40 jungle courses from full speed and from a standing start. Jungle **rivers** (`RIVER`, small 8-12, medium 13-19 or big 20-28 tiles) are crossed on logs that drift left with the current: they bob up at the far bank and sink at the near one, worked out from the clock like the moving platforms so both players see the same logs. Fall in and the current carries you back to the near bank (no death, just lost time). Each river's log length, gap and speed are picked so there's always a way across with time to spare: on a wide river the next log is up and steady a good second before yours reaches the near bank; on a narrow one a single log stays within an easy 4-tile hop of both banks for at least 2.5 s (the current slows down if nothing else fits). A test crosses every river on 30 courses, 4 times each, playing like a person. **Palm trees** (`COCONUT`, `PALMS`) are common, in 5 kinds (classic, leaning, royal, fan and windswept), all hung with coconuts; about one in five has one that falls. It starts wobbling when you run within 72 px of it (its shadow grows on the ground as it falls) and drops 0.35 s later: keep running flat out and it bonks you about half the time (dizzy and stuck for 1 s), ease off as it wobbles and it never does. Like the icicles, each player gets their own, and a falling one is never placed where being stunned would leave you in another danger's reach. **Flytraps** (`FLYTRAP`) are the jungle's trap, shared by both players. One never closes on its own: get within its sense range and it rears back for 0.35 s, then snaps at everything in its bite (12-16 px either side of it and 44 px up, higher than any jump). The sense range is worked out from the wind-up so that running straight in at full speed always gets you bitten, but backing off as soon as it rears (within about a quarter of a second) always makes it miss. After a miss it chews on nothing for 1.2 s and takes 0.35 s to open: time to run past. A victim is chewed for 1.5 s (legs kicking out of the top); then a small green one spits them back about 5 tiles (dazed for 0.8 s) and a big purple one (only after 400 m) swallows them. Each needs 9 tiles of flat, open, safe ground both sides (to bait it from and land on). A test in the real game checks that running in and jumping at any distance always gets you eaten, and faking it out works. Wild: a 0.28 s wind-up and 0.9 s of chewing on nothing, and more big ones. **Ancient turrets** (`TURRET`) are stone idol heads buried flush with the jungle floor, with nothing showing: when someone comes within 260 px of one ahead (or has run 16-40 px past one behind, about half of them), the ground rumbles for 0.45 s and it grinds up and shoots arrows along the ground. For a regular shot it rises 20 px in 0.35 s, the tip glows for 0.45 s and the arrow flies at body height at 420 px/s; for a low one it rises 16 px in 0.2 s, glows for just 0.2 s and fires at 520 px/s, skimming your feet. A jump clears either. Arrows drop as they fly (`TURRET.drop`) and stick in the ground where they land (a regular one about 270 px out, a low one about 200), harmless, then fade. Before 400 m every arrow is a plain one (red, kills you); after that it's any of plain, poison (green: 6 s of your view wobbling, doubling and changing color, worse and worse, then clearing; it never hurts you), sleep (blue: 3 s asleep on the spot) or blunt (yellow knob: thrown back like the hand's backhand, about 25 tiles, and dizzy). One fires 1-2 arrows, then sinks back for good. Its arrows are picked when it wakes up and fired in order: sleep darts first (so the next arrow finds you asleep), blunt ones last (knocking you out of reach of the rest); it only takes another shot at someone still at least 60 px from its mouth (never point-blank). Turrets are shared, and their arrows hit anyone in the way. Each sits in its own section of flat ground covering where its arrows fly, which counts as threatened, so nothing else is placed there. Tests in the real game: jumping 0-0.4 s after a low shot's glow (0.15-0.65 s for a regular one) always dodges it, and runners that jump the arrows are never hit in Normal; in Wild they need short hops to get through the quicker follow-up shots. In the **snow**, spikes are taller ice spikes, ice patches are slippery (`SNOW.iceAccel`, `SNOW.iceFriction`), icicles under snowy ledges shake and drop when you get close, and snowballs roll at you and grow (up to 17 px across, `SNOW.snowballMaxR`, so they can always be jumped). Like the crumbling platforms, each player gets their own icicles and snowballs.
- **Desert.** Spikes become cactuses (14-18 px tall, only in groups of 1-2). Quicksand pits: you sink in 3 s; about 6 quick jump presses (4+ a second) pull you free, slow pressing or wading doesn't. Tumbleweeds roll at you bouncing up and down. Settings in `DESERT` in config.js.
- **Special dangers start late.** Nothing biome-special comes before 400 m. From 400 m: black missiles, brown and black bears, brown vultures. From 500 m: red-striped missiles, polar bears and black vultures too (`THREATS` in config.js).
- **Wild mode** (`WILD` in config.js) is the hard mode, picked with the Mode button (remembered by the browser, `fire-run-mode`). Everything above comes from the very start (no 400 m / 500 m wait). Instead of a couple per biome, a missile comes for the leader the whole time they're in the prairie, the next one as soon as the last has exploded or flown off (and a death doesn't send an extra one). In the desert a new group of vultures comes 0.6 s after the last one is gone, and in the snow a new bear (any kind) charges in 0.5 s after the last one gives up, so there's always one after you; in the jungle a new jaguar or panther comes 0.6 s after the last one gives up, and turrets fire 3 arrows with less warning (0.3 s, or 0.14 s for a low shot) and less time between them. Flytraps rear back for 0.28 s instead of 0.35 and chew on nothing for 0.9 s instead of 1.2. Saws, maces and spike shooters move 15% faster (`WILD.speedUp`), large traps and sinkholes are twice as likely as each smaller size (`WILD.bigOdds`), and quicksand swallows you in 1.6 s with presses wearing off faster, so it takes about 6 presses a second to get out (Normal: about 4). The same never-get-stuck rules and tests apply: every saw still has a way through, and a simulated runner gets through Wild courses as often as Normal ones. In 1 player, Wild keeps its own best distance (`fire-run-best-wild`).
- **Each biome has one special danger.** The jungle has **jaguars** (`JAGUAR`): once or twice per jungle biome (from 400 m; black panthers from 500 m too) its small glowing eyes fade in (over 0.8 s) in one of the low bushes all through the jungle's background, 170-210 px ahead of the leader, and 1.8 s after they start to appear they narrow and then it leaps out of the background straight at the leader, following them through the air (changing speed doesn't help; its shadow follows you), comes in low and lunges right through where they are: jump as it lunges and it passes under you. Then it runs at you and attacks: crouched flat it pounces low (jump it), rear and tail up it leaps over you (keep running; jumping into it is what gets you). A jaguar attacks 3 times, a panther once but faster. It never springs from closer than 36 px (if you're running at it, it backs off first), so you always get a fair look at its crouch. If you die to something else it waits just in front of where you'll come back. Every 4-10 s, harmless decoy eyes fade in, blink and fade out in some other bush, so you can't be sure; only a jaguar's eyes narrow. A test in the real game has a player who reacts like a person (0.2-0.3 s) survive every jaguar and panther in 2 minutes of Wild, while one who never stops or always jumps dies to nearly every one. The desert has **vultures**: about every 200 m, a group of 1-3 comes for the leader. Brown ones circle for 2 s, then swoop and skim the ground (jump over them) and snatch you back about 13 tiles if they hit. Black ones dive straight in, faster, and kill. If you die while they're still circling (or haven't come in yet), they don't give up: they find where you respawn and carry on, their countdown paused while you're gone. Settings in `VULTURE`. The prairie has the missiles. The snow has **bears**: twice per snow biome (at least 70 m apart, and never two chasing at once) a bear (randomly polar, brown or black) charges in from the left after the leader. Running right nonstop always stays just ahead. Polar: fastest, 5 s, any slowdown is deadly. Brown: 10 s, a short stop is OK. Black: slowest, 15 s, catches you with honey (slowed for 4 s) instead of killing. If the player it's chasing dies to something else, the bear runs (forwards or backwards) to just behind where they'll respawn, waits in view, and carries on the chase; its clock keeps running, so if time runs out while it waits, it gives up. It waits as close as `BEAR.waitBehind` but never so close that it could catch someone running flat out before its time is up. A bear never pops into view on the trailing player's screen: if they're close enough to see where it starts, it starts off the left of their screen instead, charges through (launching them into the air if it runs over them) and only starts its timed chase once it reaches the leader's screen. Tune them in `BEAR` in config.js.
- **Missiles aren't part of the course.** After 400 m, in the prairie only. Each prairie biome gets 2 missiles 75% of the time (1 missile 15%, 3 missiles 10%, `MISSILE.perBiome`), spread out across it; with biomes off, every 350 m of prairie counts as one biome. A missile comes when the leader reaches its spot, and only one is ever in the air at a time. If the player a missile is coming for dies from something else, the missile doesn't go away or wait: it stays where it is and fires on time, at the spot where they died if they're still dead, or at them if they've already respawned. And a fresh missile comes for them once they're back (dying to a missile never sends another). It zooms in from the background on the left or right of a player, locks on for 0.6 s (missiles only ever come for the player in the lead), then fires in a straight line. Red-striped ones (after 500 m) aim where the player is heading.
- **Per-player crumbling platforms.** Each player has their own copy of the crumbling platforms. If one falls under Red, it's still there when Blue arrives.
- **Jungle orbs** (`MONSOON`, `MONKEY`): only placed in the jungle, about 15% of its orbs each. The gold monsoon works at once: for 10 s (rising over 1.2 s, draining over 1.2 s) the water rises to 30 px from the top of the screen, above every obstacle, with logs (4-5 tiles, 3-4 tile gaps) drifting left on it at 42 px/s once it's up. A log rises up under everyone's feet and lifts them. While it's flooded nothing can hurt you (`Player.die` does nothing and the hazards aren't checked), the animals, missiles and arrows are washed away, fires are put out, and the other dangers wait. Off a log you're swept left at 240 px/s for at least 0.8 s (about 24 tiles), then catch the first log you come to. As it drains, a log with someone on it carries them down onto the nearest safe ground within 60 tiles. Tests on 15 real jungle courses: standing still on your log or falling in, nobody died; a runner hopping from log to log got about 90 tiles further. The light blue monkey (S / Down) swings in on a vine from above the top of the screen (100 px radius, 0.7 s, fastest at the bottom) aimed at where the other player will be; it can only catch them near the bottom of the swing, so jumping it dodges it. Caught, they're swept up and flung back about 6 tiles. Either way it then chases them at running speed (giving up after 8 s); once it reaches them it sits on their head and covers their screen with two big hands for 4 s (`Player.blind`). Alone, it swings you forward to the next safe spot at least 8 tiles ahead (13 tiles in a test).
- **The sleep mushroom and the enchanted forest** (`SLEEP_SHROOM`, `FOREST`, `SPORES`): the main course gets a giant sleep mushroom about once per biome (`Level.sleepAt`, never before 500 m). It looks like a spore mushroom, a bit bigger and purple. Its puffs are worked out from the clock (`Level.sleepPhase`): 3-10 s on, then 10 s off. While it puffs, 12 spores a second drop from under its cap and float down at 14 px/s, drifting 9 px/s to one side (`Level.sleepSpores`), so the rain takes about 2 s to reach the ground; a spore touching you puts you to sleep (`updateSleepShrooms`). Running through once the rain is down: asleep 30 times out of 30. The forest is a separate `Level` (biome `forest`, `realmEnd` set) covering the same x as the main course from where the mushroom stands, 100-250 tiles long, shared by everyone who sleeps at that mushroom. A player in it has `realm` set: they update on that level and see it on their screen, and while the main course's dangers update, anyone in a forest (or on their way between) counts as `caught` for a moment (`onCourse`), so nothing there targets them. At `realmEnd`, fireflies swarm round you (1.3 s) and you wake back on the main course at the nearest safe spot (`moveTo`). The fire is the same fire everywhere. In the forest: 1 in 5 saws breaks loose (once it would land with 12 tiles of clear ground to roll on) and rolls at the nearest runner at 140 px/s; spike shooters float 2-10 tiles each way and can be ridden, sometimes stacked 2-3 high; about 4 spore mushrooms per forest (red 1.4x speed, blue 1.5x jump height, green poison, 10 s; pink: float up at 14 px/s for 5 s), caught about 9 times in 10 when running under one. Its obstacles have their own look (mossy logs, glowing mushroom caps, rotten branches, thorn wheels, seed-pod maces, hollow stumps). No orbs in the forest.
- **Purple catch-up orbs** (`CATCHUP`, `PORTAL_GUN`, `GRAPPLE`, `VINE`): not part of the course. The game drops them in (`updateCatchup`) for a runner who is off the leader's screen (alone: with the fire within 140 px), on safe ground ahead of them, 24 px outside both screens, so none can appear while you're less than about 430 px behind. One comes every 9 s just off the screen, down to every 3 s at 1200 px or more behind, with at most 2 waiting; anyone can grab one. Portal gun (S / Down): into a portal and out on safe ground past the next 3 obstacles (pits, maces, saws, shooters, traps, turrets...; spikes or quicksand only when 3+ tiles; dangers within 4 tiles count as one). Tests on 24 real courses: 38-140 tiles, never landing in danger. Grapple (S / Down): a hook flies at 1400 px/s to whoever is ahead and reels them back at 700 px/s to safe ground near halfway between you; used by the leader, it does nothing and you keep it. Vine (at once): swings you up and flings you over the top of the screen to where the other player was when you grabbed it (if they were dead then, to where they'd come back), and lets go just above that spot; it doesn't follow them. In the lead, or alone, it flings you 1300-1600 px (about 160-200 tiles) forward instead and lets go above whatever's there, pit or spikes included. The flight takes at least 1.4 s and never goes faster than 1100 px/s, so the camera keeps up. Alone: portal gun and vine only.
- **Snow orbs** (`SNOWSTORM`, `SNOWBALL`): only placed in the snow, about 15% of its orbs each. The gold snowstorm works at once: for 10 s a blizzard whites out every screen, and once it's blowing hard (after 0.5 s) anyone not holding left or right for 0.5 s freezes solid (`Player.stormIce`; jumping doesn't count, and being stunned, frozen, asleep or stuck in quicksand, a sinkhole or a river pauses it). Frozen, every jump cracks the ice and the third breaks you out; the ice stays until you do, even after the storm. The light blue snowball (S / Down) is thrown the way you face in a low lob (160 px/s, 70 up) and lands about 7 tiles away; its 3 px hit box freezes the other player for 7 s (`Player.iceLock`: no input works), never the thrower, and a jump clears it. Alone, the snowball orb gives you 20 s of skates instead: on ice you run 1.5 times as fast and speed up 4 times as quickly (in a test, 54 tiles in 3 s on ice against 35).
- **Desert orbs** (`SANDSTORM`, `FIREHAWK`): only placed in the desert, about 15% of its orbs each. The gold sandstorm works at once: for 10 s (building up and dying down over 1 s) every player is pushed left at 34 px/s (whoever grabbed it at 90% of that), against running at 96, and sand blows across the screens. In a test, 10 s of running covered 87 tiles for the one who grabbed it and 83 for the other, against 120 with no storm; standing still drifts you back about 38 tiles. The light blue firehawk (S / Down) flies out at 300 px/s to the point above the other player where a stick dropped from 48 px up would land on them (it leads them by their speed) and lets go. The stick kills anyone it hits, and the ground where it lands burns for 10 s, 32 px across and 8 px high: touch it and you die; a running jump clears it if you take off 4-16 px before it. Respawning never puts you in a fire. Alone, the firehawk comes out as soon as you grab it and circles you; the first vulture, bear or jaguar that comes within 150 px of you, or missile locked on to you within 230 px, it dives on and burns, then it flies off.
- **How many orbs:** about 3 per biome on average in every biome (`ORBS`): never closer than 30 tiles apart, and after that each new section has a per-biome chance of being an orb (each biome has a different mix of sections, so each has its own weight). Over 240 courses: prairie 3.1, desert 3.0, snow 3.1, jungle 3.1 per biome; about 1 in 50 biomes gets none.
- **Orbs work once.** When anyone touches a swap orb, the players trade places, including everything about where they are. About half the orbs are freeze orbs in disguise (the arrows in the middle are flipped): they hold whoever touches them still for 3 seconds. Either way the orb disappears for both players. Dash orbs (marked with a ») hold you in the air for a second, then launch you far to the right, wherever that lands you. Light blue orbs are the ones you use with S / Down. The drone orb sends a rescue drone: used by the player behind, it grabs the leader and drops them just in front of you; used by the leader, it catches you once if you fall into a pit. In 1 player the drone starts guarding as soon as you pick it up, no button needed (`DRONE` in config.js). Wind dash orbs (one white arrow, with white wind blowing off them) are the most common: press S (Red) or Down (Blue) to instantly dash about 12 tiles the way you're facing, on the ground or in the air. Lightning orbs (a white zigzag) give you one shot: press S (Red) or Down (Blue) to fire a bolt along the ground in the direction you're facing when you shoot. It travels the shared course until it reaches the other player, who has to jump over it. Hand orbs (a purple hand) give you a short-range mystic hand on the same key: facing right it grabs the other player and suplexes them backwards, facing left it backhands them further back. You hold one power at a time; a new one replaces the old.

**After changing the game:** the script and style links in `index.html` end in `?v=` and a number. Change that number (for example to today's date and time) whenever you publish an update, so browsers load the new files instead of old saved copies.
