Fire Run

A 2-player race across an endless prairie. Same course, two runners, and a wall of fire that keeps getting faster.

Fire Run needs a keyboard — two players share one.

How to play

Red (top screen)

W jump · S use ability

A D run left / right

Blue (bottom screen)

↑ jump · ↓ use ability

← → run left / right

Both runners race through the exact same course. Keep running right — a wall of fire is chasing each of you, and it gets faster and faster. If the fire catches you, the other player wins.

  • Biomes — off at first, so the whole race stays in the prairie while you learn the game. Turn them on with the Biomes button under the start buttons, then use the little arrow next to it to choose which biomes you get (pick just one and the whole race is in it). Then the course changes as you run: about every 350 m a wooden sign marks a new biome. The first race starts in the green prairie; after that a race can start anywhere.
  • Wild mode — the hard mode. Pick it with the Mode button (it switches between Normal and Wild). In Wild there's no calm start: every danger comes right away, not just after 400 m. In the prairie a missile comes for the leader as soon as the last one is gone. In the desert vultures never stop: when one group is done, the next one comes. In the snow a bear is always chasing: escape one and the next charges straight in. In the jungle a jaguar or panther is always hunting you, flytraps snap quicker, and ancient turrets fire 3 arrows with less warning (tap jump for short hops to dodge them all). Saws, maces and spike shooters are a bit faster, big deadly traps and sinkholes are more common, and quicksand pulls you under in under 2 seconds, so you have to really mash jump to get out. Playing alone, your best Wild distance is kept separately.
  • Thin ice (snow) — pale, frosty patches over a frozen pond. The first time anyone walks on it, it cracks, and the cracks stay. Walking across or standing on it is fine, but step back onto cracked ice, jump on or off it, or have both players stand on it at once, and it breaks: you're frozen in a block of ice. Press jump 3 times to break out. If both players are frozen in the same pond, the first one out survives and the other's ice shatters with them inside. The water stays open afterwards, so walking in freezes you again.
  • Snow biome — the spikes are tall ice spikes. Shiny blue ice is slippery: you keep sliding when you let go. Icicles hang under snowy ledges: when they start shaking, slow down, because they're about to drop (and don't jump into them). Snowballs roll at you and grow as they roll, so jump over them.
  • Sinkholes (desert) — little holes in the sand, hard to spot. Step too close and a small or medium one spins open and pulls you in. Mashing jump won't help: wait for the sand to stop pouring for a moment (it sparkles), then jump out. A large one (after 400 m) caves in to a whole gap: jump right over the hole or you fall. Once a sinkhole opens it stays open for both players.
  • Desert biome — the spikes are cactuses: much taller (hard to jump), but only one or two at a time. Darker, swirling quicksand pulls you under in 3 seconds: mash jump (about 4+ times a second, 6+ in Wild mode) to struggle free, or just jump over it. Tumbleweeds bounce towards you as they roll, so time your jump.
  • Jungle biome — the spikes come in two kinds: bamboo stakes, thin but very tall (time your jump to clear the tallest ones), and thorn bushes, low but really wide (jump from close to the edge). Rivers are too wide to jump: hop across on the logs drifting down them. The logs bob up at the far bank, drift back towards you and sink at the near bank, so don't ride one too long. Fall in and the current carries you all the way back to the start of the river. Palm trees are everywhere, full of coconuts. Most never fall, but when one starts to wobble (and a shadow appears on the ground), ease off: it drops, and if it bonks you, you're dizzy for a second.
  • Flytraps (jungle) — giant snapping plants. You can't run past one or jump over it: get close and it rears back, then snaps at everything in reach, even high in the air. Fake it out: step into its range, then back off right away. It snaps on nothing and chews air for a moment, so run past while it's busy. If it gets you, it chews you for a bit, then a green one spits you back out; a purple one (after 400 m) swallows you. While it's chewing someone, the other player can run straight past.
  • Ancient turrets (jungle) — stone idol heads buried under the jungle floor, with no sign of them at all. When the ground rumbles, one is coming up: some rise ahead of you and shoot back at you, some wait until you've run right over them and rise up behind you. It shoots fast arrows along the ground, either at body height or low at your feet: a jump always clears them. A low shot comes quicker, with less warning, and flies even faster. Arrows drop as they fly and stick in the ground where they land, so they only reach so far. Just before each shot, its eyes and the arrow's tip glow the arrow's color. Red: a plain arrow that kills you (the only kind before 400 m). Green: a poison dart that makes your screen stranger and stranger for a few seconds. Blue: a sleep dart, fast asleep on the spot for 3 seconds. Yellow knob: a blunt arrow that knocks you back as far as the hand's backhand. A turret fires once or twice, then sinks back for good. When it fires more than once, a sleep dart always comes first (the next arrow finds you asleep) and a blunt arrow always comes last. It's shared: its arrows hit anyone in the way.
  • Jaguars — the jungle's special danger. Once or twice in every jungle biome, small glowing eyes slowly fade in, in one of the bushes in the background ahead of whoever is in the lead (watch the bushes: sometimes harmless eyes blink there too, but those never narrow). When they narrow, it leaps out straight at you, following you through the air, and comes in low: jump as it lunges and it shoots right under you. Then it comes after you and attacks again. Watch how it crouches: flat to the ground, it pounces low at you, so jump; rear and tail up, it leaps over you, so keep running and don't jump. A jaguar attacks 3 times; a black panther (after 500 m, with green eyes) is faster but attacks only once. If you die to something else, it waits for you to come back.
  • Vultures — the desert's special danger. Every so often a group of 1 to 3 comes for whoever is in the lead. A brown vulture circles above you for a couple of seconds, then swoops down and skims the ground at your feet: jump over it, or it snatches you and carries you back. A black vulture doesn't circle: it dives straight in, faster, and kills you if it hits. Watch its shadow on the ground.
  • Bears — the snow's special danger. Twice in every snow biome, a bear charges in from the left (never two at once: the second waits until the first chase is over) with a roar, chasing whoever is in the lead. Keep running right! A polar bear chases for 5 seconds and catches you if you slow down at all. A brown bear chases for 10 seconds; you can afford a quick stop. A black bear chases for 15 seconds, and if it catches you it covers you in honey, slowing you down for a few seconds. If you die to something else during the chase, the bear doesn't give up: it runs (forwards or back) to where you'll respawn, waits for you and carries on chasing until its time is up. If a bear runs over the other player on its way, it launches them high into the air.
  • Spikes and pits — touch one and you'll come back a little way behind after a second. That's time the fire uses to catch up.
  • Moving platforms — slide back and forth over big gaps. Hop on and ride across.
  • Crumbling platforms — shake when you land on them, then fall. Keep moving!
  • Portals — a blue portal on a high ledge teleports you to the orange one ahead, skipping a spiky stretch.
  • Spike shooters — stone blocks that fire 4 spikes diagonally every couple of seconds. They sit on the ground, float at head height or hang overhead. Their red core glows just before they fire, so time your run.
  • Missiles — the rarest danger, after 400 m, and only in the prairie. One zooms in from far away in the background, from the left or the right, then locks on to the player in the lead: a red target flashes on them and a dotted line shows where it will fly. Then it fires in a straight line. Black missiles aim where you are; red-striped ones (after 500 m) aim where you're going to be, so change your speed or jump. Stay clear of the explosion. Dying to something else before it lands doesn't save you: it still fires (where you were, or at you if you're already back), and another one comes for you when you respawn.
  • Saw blades — spinning saws in small, medium and large, each at its own speed. Some slide back and forth in a slot in the ground (jump over them), some go up and down a rail, rising high enough to hit you mid-jump (run under when it's up, or jump it while it's down in the ground), and some rise out of pits (jump across while it's down).
  • Bear traps (prairie) — they lie flat in the grass anywhere on the course, so look closely. Step on one and it snaps shut. Get out of a small or medium trap by wiggling: press left and right back and forth (4 times for small, 8 for medium); it won't let go on its own. If the other player runs into you while you're stuck, you're knocked free, but they get caught in the trap instead! A large trap (with a red plate, only after 400 m) kills you. Once a trap has snapped it stays shut, so nobody can step in it again. Jump over them!
  • Bushes (prairie) — step inside a bush and you're hidden: nobody can see you on either screen. But 1 bush in 10 has a trap hidden inside, and once it snaps, the bush is torn open and you can be seen again.
  • Swinging maces — spiked balls swinging on chains from the sky, each at its own speed. Run past while it's swung away, or jump right over it.
  • Orb colors — gold orbs work the moment you touch them. Light blue orbs give you an ability to use when you want, with S (Red) or ↓ (Blue).
  • Golden orbs — floating in the air. Some swap you and the other player's places. Others freeze you for 3 seconds. They look almost the same, so look closely before you jump into one. Each orb only works once.
  • Dash orbs — golden orbs with a » in the middle. Grab one and you hang in the air for a second, then get launched far to the right. Where you land is up to luck.
  • Wind dash orbs — light blue orbs with one white arrow and white wind blowing off them. Grab one, then press S (Red) or ↓ (Blue) to instantly whoosh about 12 tiles the way you're facing. It works in the air too, so use it to cross a gap, dodge something, or get ahead.
  • Lightning orbs — light blue orbs with a white zigzag. Grab one to store a lightning shot, then press S (Red) or ↓ (Blue) to fire it along the ground. It flies the way you're facing when you shoot, so turn around (press left) to shoot back at a player behind you. It travels to the other player wherever they are. Jump over it, or get zapped back.
  • Drone orbs — light blue orbs with a little white drone. Press S / ↓ to send out a rescue drone. If you're behind, it flies to the player in the lead, grabs them and drops them safely just in front of you. If you're in the lead, it follows you and catches you once if you fall into a pit, putting you down past it. Playing alone, the drone does this as soon as you pick it up, no button needed.
  • Hand orbs — light blue orbs with a purple hand. Grab one, then press S (Red) or ↓ (Blue) to throw a mystic hand a short way in the direction you're facing. Facing right, it grabs the other player and suplexes them backwards. Facing left, it backhands them further back. It flies in a straight line, so jump over it or duck under it. You hold one power at a time: a new wind dash, lightning or hand orb replaces the old one.
  • Jungle orbs — two orbs found only in the jungle. The gold monsoon orb (raindrops on a wave) works the moment you grab it: pouring rain, and a fast river rises up over everything, above every obstacle, for 10 seconds. Nothing can hurt you while it's flooded: just stay on the logs drifting along it, hopping from one to the next. Fall in and the current sweeps you back, fast, until you catch a log again. When it drains, the log you're on carries you down onto safe ground. The light blue monkey orb (a monkey's face) gives you a monkey: press S (Red) or ↓ (Blue) and it swings in fast from the top of the screen on a vine, sweeping through the other player. If it catches them it scoops them up and flings them a little back; caught or not, it then jumps off its vine and chases them at running speed, and once it gets them it sits on their head and covers their eyes with two big hands for 4 seconds. Jump the swing, and keep running! Playing alone, the monkey swings you forward to the next safe spot instead.
  • Purple orbs — catch-up orbs that only come for whoever has fallen behind: off the leader's screen (playing alone: with the fire on your screen). They appear up ahead of you, out of sight of both players, and the further behind you are, the more often they come (anyone can grab one, though). The portal gun (a swirling portal): press S (Red) or ↓ (Blue) and you go into a portal and come out of another one on safe ground past the next 3 big obstacles (a single spike doesn't count). The grapple (a wavy rope): press S / ↓ and a rope flies to whoever is ahead of you, hooks them and reels them back, halfway to you. The vine (a vine with a leaf) works the moment you grab it: it swings you up and flings you to where the other player was when you grabbed it (if they keep running, they'll be a little ahead of you when you land; whatever's there by then is your problem). If you're the one in front, or playing alone, it flings you far ahead instead, further than any portal, and lands you wherever it lands you, even in a pit. Playing alone, there's no grapple.
  • Snow orbs — two orbs found only in the snow. The gold snowstorm orb (a snowflake) works the moment you grab it: for 10 seconds a blizzard whites out everyone's screen, and anyone who isn't holding left or right freezes solid within half a second, in a block of ice like the thin ice (jumping doesn't count as moving). Jump 3 times to break out. The light blue snowball orb gives you a snowball: press S (Red) or ↓ (Blue) to throw it the way you're facing. It's small and only goes about 7 tiles, but if it hits the other player they're frozen for 7 seconds, with no way out. Jump it! Playing alone, the snowball orb gives you skates instead: 20 seconds of running faster on ice.
  • Desert orbs — two orbs found only in the desert. The gold sandstorm orb (sand streaks) works the moment you grab it: for 10 seconds sand blows across everyone's screen, making things a little harder to see, and slowly pushes everyone left, so running is harder and standing still blows you back. Whoever grabbed it is pushed a little less. The light blue firehawk orb (a little orange bird) gives you a firehawk: press S (Red) or ↓ (Blue) and it flies out over the other player and drops a burning stick where they're going to be. The stick kills if it hits, and the ground it lands on burns for 10 seconds: touch the flames and you die, but they're low, so a running jump clears them. Change your speed when it drops! Playing alone, the firehawk comes out by itself and circles you, and the first animal that comes for you (a vulture, bear or jaguar), or a missile locked on to you, it dives on and burns.

1 Player: race the fire on your own, with WASD or the arrow keys, and see how far you get. Only the dash, wind dash and drone orbs appear (the others only matter against another player), plus the monsoon and monkey (which swings you forward) in the jungle, the snowstorm and snowball (skates) in the snow, and the sandstorm and firehawk (which guards you) in the desert. With the fire on your screen, purple portal gun and vine orbs come to help.

Press P or Esc to pause. Press F or the Fullscreen button to make the game fill the screen. Press M or the Sound button to turn the sound off or on.

About

A practice project. The course is generated as you play, so every race is different and it never ends.
